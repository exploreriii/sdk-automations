# 6.10 The two destructive writes

Two writes the destructive ladder already decides, and, when this was written,
could not send. The two adapter transports declared no endpoint and no verb, and
the two shell handlers refused at the send for the reason the matrix gave: no
confirmed row named either. Core already stated their facts —
`clockTriggeredDestructive`, `pull_requests:write` and `issues:write` — so the
only thing missing was contact with GitHub.

**Run 2026-09-12 cited both, and the lead reversed both by hand.** A write is
confirmed here or the shell keeps refusing it. Nothing below is settled by
reasoning about GitHub's documentation.

## Question

That the two writes the destructive ladder decides can be sent, verified by
readback, and reversed by a human, under the App's existing grants, in one call
each — and what the failure shapes are without the grant.

| Write | Endpoint | Grant | Readback | Reversal (by the lead, as a human) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| close a pull request | `PATCH /repos/{o}/{r}/pulls/{n}` body `{ state: "closed" }` | Pull requests W | `GET …/pulls/{n}`: `state`, `closed_at`, `closed_by.login` — must name the App | `gh pr reopen` |
| release an assignment | `DELETE /repos/{o}/{r}/issues/{n}/assignees` body `{ assignees: [login] }` | Issues W | `GET …/issues/{n}`: `assignees[].login` no longer holds the login | `gh issue edit --add-assignee` |

## Red line

**Two writes, one item each, App-authored, and both reversed by a human before
this protocol closes.** Each write is sent ONCE; there is no retry, ever, on a
write — a retry would replace the failure shape the protocol exists to observe
and, on a destructive verb, would be the second act nobody decided.

The runner performs the write, the readback, and then nothing. The reversal is
the lead's, by hand, so the protocol also records that a human can undo the
App: the summary prints the two `gh` commands and sends neither.

The App is given no `contents: write` — the ceiling in
`design/findings/endpoint-permission-matrix.md` withholds it deliberately — and
nothing here needs it. Personal sandbox only. No token, header or key is
written anywhere; the evidence log keeps only the headers it already harvests.

## Method

### The state the sandbox must hold

| Fixture | What it is | Made by |
|---|---|---|
| `PR_TO_CLOSE` | an open, unmerged probe pull request the App may close; App-authored comments on it are fine | **the lead** — the App holds `contents: read` and cannot create a branch |
| `ISSUE_TO_RELEASE` | an open issue with exactly ONE assignee | **the lead** — the login must be a real account, and a second account is not something a token creates |

The login the release names is read off the issue by the `state` step, not
supplied in the environment: a release that names a login the item does not
hold measures nothing. Exactly one assignee is required, so that
`assignees[].login` going empty is the whole readback.

### How the calls are made

- **Each write is sent once, as one plain `fetch`.** The measured calls do not
  travel through Octokit: its throttling and retry plugins absorb exactly the
  signals under observation (6.4).
- **The readback is unpaced**, immediately after the write, and then again
  after two seconds — write-to-visible, on the scale 6.7 measured for comments.
  Every other call is paced two seconds apart.
- **The runner reads both fixtures first and refuses on mismatch**, naming the
  fixture: a closed pull request, a closed issue, or an assignee count that is
  not one stops the run before anything is written.
- **`--plan` resolves the environment and prints every step, its endpoint, its
  verb and the readback fields, without a single request.** The key file is
  reported as present or absent and never opened.

Evidence lands in `evidence/6.10-destructive-writes-<run>.jsonl`; headers are
redacted as 6.9 does. `harness/src/run-6.10.ts` is the runner.

## Steps

| # | Step | Sends | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `state` | `GET …/pulls/{n}` and `GET …/issues/{n}` | what the lead's fixtures actually hold, the assignee the release will name, and the refusal when either is wrong |
| 2 | `closePullRequest` | one `PATCH …/pulls/{n}`, once | that the close lands in one call under Pull requests W, and **that `closed_by.login` names the App** — the part that makes the act auditable rather than anonymous |
| 3 | `releaseAssignment` | one `DELETE …/issues/{n}/assignees`, once | that a body-carrying DELETE takes one named login off, that the other assignees are untouched, and what the endpoint returns when it does |
| 4 | `negative` | the same two writes, once each, on a token minted `issues: read` and `pull_requests: read` | the permission each write actually needs, from the 403's `x-accepted-github-permissions` |
| 5 | `summary` | nothing | the evidence table's rows, and the two `gh` commands the lead reverses with |

Step 4 is why the permission column can be filled at all, and it runs last
deliberately: a 403 precedes any state check, so the fixtures the earlier steps
already changed do not alter the failure shape. The general 403 shape is not
re-measured; it is in the failure catalogue from 6.1 and 6.4.

### What this run found

- **The two writes land in one call each, and the read-back sees them at once** —
  immediately after the write and again two seconds later, on both. Neither
  needed a retry, and neither was given one.
- **`closed_by` is absent from the pull object.** The actor of a close is on the
  timeline — `closed by automation-experiment-3892384[bot]` on the `closed`
  event — and nowhere on the item, so **authorship of a close is read from the
  timeline, never from the pull object**. A read-back that wanted `closed_by`
  would never confirm; the state is what confirms.
- **An assignment event names the assignee as its actor**: `assigned exploreriii
  by exploreriii` for the seed's App-made assignment, and `unassigned
  exploreriii by exploreriii` for the App's release. An App-made release is
  indistinguishable from a human's on the timeline by actor alone, so any rule
  reading "a human reversed the App" off an assignment event must not rely on
  the actor.
- **The release's 403 names two permissions**, `issues=write; pull_requests=write`,
  where the close's names one. The issue surface is what the operation declares
  and what it was sent under.

### The reversal

Both by the lead, by hand, after the readbacks: the runner sent neither. Each
was observed on its item's timeline.

| Write | Reversed by | Observed |
|---|---|---|
| the close | `gh pr reopen 181` | #181 open again |
| the release | `gh issue edit 184 --add-assignee exploreriii` | #184 `assignees=[exploreriii]` |

## What migrates

Conclusions never live here. A confirmed run moves:

**Two rows into the endpoint-permission matrix**, in the Operations table —
Close a pull request (`PATCH /repos/{o}/{r}/pulls/{n}`, Pull requests W) and
Release one assignment (`DELETE /repos/{o}/{r}/issues/{n}/assignees`, Issues
W). Each carries its permission from step 4 and its citation from the run, and
each states what its readback can and cannot establish: the close's row that
`closed_by` is absent and the actor is the timeline's, the release's that an
assignment event's actor is the assignee either way.

**The two adapter transports gain their endpoint and verb.**
`packages/runtime/src/adapter/operations/closePullRequest.ts` and
`packages/runtime/src/adapter/operations/releaseAssignment.ts` stop being
empty: one endpoint shape each, matched structurally on method and path tail,
one verb each, and their two names join the `WriteEndpoint` union in
`packages/runtime/src/adapter/operations/transport.ts`. Both verbs are
idempotency class `idempotent`, as
`packages/core/src/intents/operations/closePullRequest.ts` and
`packages/core/src/intents/operations/releaseAssignment.ts` already declare.

**The design pages of inactivity drop "refused at the send."** The two shell
handlers' `send` stops answering `forbidden` and their `confirm` becomes the
readback this protocol pins; `packages/capabilities/src/inactivity/design.md`
phases 2 and 3 lose the endpoint half of what they need first, and
`design/guides/grace.md` §3's act-then-notice plan has an act to run.

## Not decided by this protocol

**The other four refused verbs.** `assign`, `unassign`, `lockIssue` and
`unlockIssue` stay refused. `unassign` is close to step 3 and is not the same
write — it takes an item's assignees whole rather than one named login — and
the rest are separate endpoints. One measured write confirms one row.

**Whether the ladder may run armed.** The armed FX-gate run
(`design/guides/write-operations.md` §1's standing obligation, protocol 8.2)
has not been made on this path, and a confirmed endpoint does not substitute
for it. This protocol proves the send; 8.2 proves the platform around it.
